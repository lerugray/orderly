          What he handles

          Each duty carries the privilege it actually runs at. Where a scope says read only, sending is switched off underneath it, not just discouraged in the prompt.

          • Mail triage Reads both inboxes — personal and work — summarises what came in, says what needs you, and always names which account it came to. Read only
          • Reply drafts Writes the reply into the drafts of whichever account it answers. You read it and press send. Drafts only
          • Approval queue Everything he finishes waits here. A draft: approving records that you read it and kept it, and you press send in Gmail. A calendar proposal: approving is the act — the event appears when you click, and not before. You decide
          • Mail routines Standing rules you write — when mail matching one arrives, he tells you, drafts a reply, or both. Nothing else: there is no rule that can file, delete or forward, because the scopes for that don't exist here. Tell or draft
          • Calendar Reads what's scheduled and answers questions about the week. He can also propose an event — new, or a change to one — and it waits on the queue above. Approving is what creates it; he holds no path to your calendar himself, and there is no delete, at any layer, for anything. You approve writes
          • Standing checks Runs on a schedule you set and reports back through Telegram. Owner sets it
          • Research Searches the web and reads pages, then comes back with the answer and the real links. Keyless search, no account, no tracker. Read only
          • Remembering Keeps what you tell him — preferences, standing instructions, facts about your setup — in notes on the station, and has them at the start of the next conversation. He writes to those notes and nothing else. Never credentials, and never what an email or a web page said about you. His notes only
          • Sending mail Blocked in the container's environment, not just in the prompt — for every account, because the block binds the process rather than the mailbox. Off

          Standing orders

          • He reads your mail. He cannot send it. Inbound email is untrusted text, so the ceiling is a draft. You send it yourself from Gmail.

          • Every tool runs in a sealed container. No network, no privileges, and a read-only disk with one exception: the coordinator can write his own notes, in his own folder, and nowhere else. His standing orders sit in that same folder owned by the host — he can read them and cannot touch them. Only the mail agent gets an outside line, and it gets it alone.

          • One operator, one box, no public door. The gateway answers on loopback and reaches you over your own network. Nothing is published to the internet.
